SOC-TRI-20261009-01 · Opened 2026-10-09

Web exposure triage: Habesha Hub community website

AssetHabesha Hub (GitHub Pages)
TriggerScheduled self-review of an owned site
MethodPassive, read-only page review
Highest severityMedium
Findings1 Medium, 6 Low
StatusOpen: remediation in progress

Summary

A passive triage of a community resource site I built and own found no signs of compromise or malicious activity. It did find seven weaknesses: one Medium (privacy policy gaps) and six Low, including missing HTTP hardening headers.

Case timeline (2026-10-09, Eastern Time)

TimeActionResult
11:54Case opened; passive review of 5 pages6 findings logged, headers marked UNKNOWN

16:10curl -sI header check from the lab Kali VMF7 confirmed Low: HSTS present, hardening headers missing

Scope and authorization

I own and maintain the site. Review was read-only: page fetches only, with no scanning, form submissions, or login attempts. Reviewed pages: homepage, Ohio directory, privacy policy, daily tools, and one empty state page.

Findings

HTTP header check (F7 evidence)

Command: curl -sI https://cybersamliya.github.io/habesha-hub/, run from the Kali VM. Response: HTTP/2 200, served by GitHub Pages through a Fastly/Varnish cache.

Indicators of compromise

None. No evidence of defacement, injected scripts, or unexpected third-party code was observed in the reviewed pages.

MITRE ATT&CK

No adversary activity was observed, so no technique is mapped as observed behavior. As a risk note only, F1 is the kind of exposure that supports Reconnaissance: Gather Victim Identity Information, Email Addresses (T1589.002).

Classification

Not an attack. These are security and quality weaknesses on an owned asset, handled as remediation items rather than an incident.

Next steps

  1. Fix F2 (privacy disclosures) and F1 (preview URL).
  2. Add a meta Content-Security-Policy (header check completed from the lab Kali VM on 2026-10-09 with curl -sI).
  3. Fix the remaining Low items, then re-run this triage to verify closure.

Lessons learned

A small static site still has an attack surface: linked URLs, third-party forms, and policy pages. Labeling each finding with a confidence level kept what I observed separate from what I assumed.

SOC-TRI-20260922-01 · Opened 2026-09-22

Alert batch triage: brute force to encoded PowerShell download cradle

Data sourceSynthetic Wazuh/Security Onion-style export
Volume25 alerts, 10 incidents
Severity mix5 Critical, 5 High, 4 Medium, 11 Low
Focus incidentIncident 4, host win-client-01
ClassificationTrue Positive (simulated)
StatusClosed: escalated to Tier 2/IR

Summary

My SOC Alert Triage Tool grouped 25 synthetic alerts into 10 incidents by source IP within a 30-minute window. I then manually investigated Incident 4, where a brute-force login was followed two minutes later by an encoded PowerShell command, and escalated it.

Incident 4 timeline (UTC, 2026-09-20)

  • 02:14:03 to 02:14:20: four failed SSH authentications from 185.220.101[.]34
  • 02:14:31: multiple failures followed by a success (rule level 10)
  • 02:16:02: powershell.exe -enc ... on win-client-01 (rule level 12)

Investigation

I decoded the base64 payload in CyberChef with From Base64 then Decode Text (UTF-16LE), matching how PowerShell's -EncodedCommand encodes scripts. It revealed IEX (New-Object Net.WebClient).DownloadString(...), a fileless download cradle that runs a remote script in memory.

The download URL pointed to the same IP that had just brute-forced the host. That link between the two events is what moved this from "suspicious" to "escalate".

Indicators of compromise (synthetic, defanged)

  • IP: 185.220.101[.]34
  • URL: hxxp://185.220.101[.]34:8080/update.ps1
  • Host: win-client-01

MITRE ATT&CK (evidence-supported only)

TacticTechniqueEvidenceConfidence
Credential AccessT1110 Brute ForceFour failed logins then a success from one IP within 28 secondsLIKELY
ExecutionT1059.001 PowerShellEncoded PowerShell command with an IEX download cradleCONFIRMED

Classification and response

True Positive within the simulated scenario. Recommended escalation to Tier 2/IR, plus a check of outbound firewall or proxy logs for connections from win-client-01 to the attacker IP after 02:16:02 to confirm whether the payload ran.

Lessons learned

Automated correlation found the pattern, but the manual decode proved the alert mattered. I scoped version 1 to source-IP correlation only and documented that limitation rather than over-building.
